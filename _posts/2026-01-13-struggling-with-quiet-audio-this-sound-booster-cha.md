## Why Turning Up the Volume Always Fails

For years, the "solution" has been painfully simplistic: just push the volume slider higher. But this traditional approach is fundamentally broken. Your device's built-in amplifier and digital-to-analog converter (DAC) are designed with strict limits to prevent damage to the hardware and your hearing. When you hit that maximum, the system starts **clipping**—chopping off the peaks of audio waves—which creates nasty distortion.

Think of it like trying to make a small flashlight brighter by overloading it. You don't get more useful light; you just blow the bulb. Similarly, forcing more power through limited speakers doesn't create richer sound; it creates a brittle, noisy mess. You might gain a decibel or two, but you sacrifice all the detail, warmth, and balance that makes listening enjoyable. It's a trade-off that leaves you with louder bad sound, not better sound.
