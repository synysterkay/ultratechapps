### Step 3: Access the Equalizer

### Step 4: Set the Preamp and Cut Sub-Bass
Before boosting, lower the **Preamp** slider (usually on the left) to -3dB to prevent clipping. Then, reduce the lowest band (32Hz) by -2dB. **Why?** Sub-bass frequencies often cause distortion on phone speakers and cheap headphones. **What you'll see:** The 32Hz slider moves down slightly. **Common error:** Skipping this step leads to muddy, distorted bass.

### Step 5: Boost Mid-Bass for Punch
Now, increase the 60Hz and 120Hz bands by +4dB and +3dB respectively. These frequencies give you the thump you feel. **What you'll see:** The sliders rise, and the overall EQ curve forms a gentle hill in the low end. **Tip:** Use the app's **Bass Boost** slider (separate from EQ) to add extra oomph – set it to 30% for now.

### Step 6: Tame the Mids and Highs
To prevent vocals from getting drowned out, slightly reduce the 250Hz and 500Hz bands by -1dB each. Leave the higher frequencies (2kHz and up) flat or boost 4kHz by +1dB for clarity. **What you'll see:** A balanced curve that emphasizes lows without sacrificing detail. **Common error:** Over-boosting highs makes bass sound weak – keep it moderate.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Boosting all bass frequencies equally** – This causes muddiness. Instead, cut sub-bass and boost mid-bass selectively.
2. **Ignoring the preamp** – Without lowering preamp, boosting bass leads to digital clipping. Always set preamp to -3dB or lower.
3. **Overusing Bass Boost slider** – The separate Bass Boost slider can overpower the EQ. Keep it under 50% for clean sound.
4. **Not testing with different genres** – Settings that work for EDM may make rock sound bloated. Create multiple presets.
5. **Forgetting to disable other EQs** – Conflicting EQs from your phone or music app can double-process audio, causing distortion.

## Advanced Tips for Power Users
