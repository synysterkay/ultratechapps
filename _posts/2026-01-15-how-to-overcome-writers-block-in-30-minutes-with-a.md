The first step is mental. Stop fighting the feeling. Acknowledge that writer's block is a normal part of the **writing process**. Tell yourself, "It's okay that I don't have the perfect opening line yet. My job right now is just to generate raw material." Set a timer for 30 minutes. This creates positive urgency and focuses your brain. Your mission is not to write the essay, but to create a map for it.

### Step 2: Dump All Your Ideas (The Brain Dump)

Open a blank document. Set a timer for 5 minutes. Now, write down EVERYTHING you know or think about the essay topic. Don't judge, don't edit, don't worry about grammar. Just vomit words onto the page. Write questions, half-baked thoughts, single words, quotes from your readings. This messy brain dump is your raw clay. It gets the stagnant ideas flowing and proves to your brain that you *do* have material to work with.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using AI for Essays

1.  **Passively Accepting the First Output:** The biggest pitfall is treating the AI as an oracle. Its first draft is a starting point, not a final product. Always refine, edit, and personalize the thesis and outline to match your voice and assignment specifics.
2.  **Skipping the Brain Dump:** Jumping straight into the app without doing your own messy thinking first makes you a passive consumer. The brain dump primes your own cognitive engine, ensuring the AI enhances *your* ideas, not replaces them.
3.  **Letting the AI Write the Essay:** Using the tool to generate full paragraphs or the entire essay is academically risky and defeats the purpose. It robs you of the learning and critical thinking process. Use it for structure and ideation, not composition.
4.  **Ignoring the Assignment Rubric:** An AI doesn't know your professor's specific guidelines. Always cross-check the generated thesis and outline against your prompt's requirements for length, source inclusion, and specific questions to answer.
