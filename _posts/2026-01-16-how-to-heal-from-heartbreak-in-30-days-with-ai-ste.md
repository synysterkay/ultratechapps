Your brain's first defense is often disbelief. Don't fight it. Your task is simply to name it. Say out loud, "I am in shock. This feels unreal." Write this down in a notes app or journal. ### Step 2: Understand the "Withdrawal" Phase (Days 4-7)

This is the science part. When a primary attachment is severed, your brain experiences a drop in dopamine and oxytocin—the chemicals associated with reward and bonding. This creates literal withdrawal symptoms: anxiety, craving, and fatigue. Knowing this is *biological*, not a sign of weakness, is powerful. It means your feelings are normal and temporary.

## 5 Common Heartbreak Healing Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Isolating Yourself:** Withdrawing from all social contact confirms the brain's fear that the lost relationship was your only source of connection. Force small, low-pressure social interactions, even if it's just a quick coffee.
2.  **Stalking Ex-Partners on Social Media:** This is neurological self-sabotage. Each view acts like a "hit" of the person, reactivating dopamine pathways and reinforcing the addiction, resetting your withdrawal clock. Use app blockers if necessary.
3.  **Bottling Up Emotions:** Suppressing feelings doesn't make them disappear; it amplifies their physical and psychological toll. The guided journaling in Fresh Start provides a safe, structured outlet for release.
4.  **Rushing into a Rebound:** Using a new person as a distraction prevents you from processing the core attachment wound. It often leads to repeating patterns. Focus on the relationship with yourself first.
5.  **Believing the Pain is Permanent:** In the thick of it, heartbreak feels endless. This is a cognitive distortion. Remind yourself of the science—this is a temporary, albeit painful, phase of recalibration.
