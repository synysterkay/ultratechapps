## Step 3: Pop Music Settings

Pop music thrives on crisp vocals and punchy bass. In Volume Booster - Sound Booster, adjust the sliders as follows:
- 60Hz: +3dB (adds warmth without boominess)
- 230Hz: +2dB (enhances body of vocals)
- 910Hz: +4dB (brings vocals forward)
- 3.6kHz: +3dB (adds clarity and presence)
- 14kHz: +2dB (adds air and sparkle)

What you'll hear: Vocals become intimate and clear, while the bassline maintains energy without overpowering the mix. Common error: Boosting 60Hz too high (+6dB or more) creates a muddy sound that masks the lead singer.

## Step 4: Rock Music Settings

Rock demands aggressive guitars and punchy drums. Use these settings in Volume Booster - Sound Booster:
- 60Hz: +2dB (tight low-end for kick drum)
- 230Hz: +1dB (slight warmth for rhythm guitars)
- 910Hz: +5dB (cuts through distortion, highlights vocals)
- 3.6kHz: +4dB (adds bite to lead guitar)
- 14kHz: +3dB (crisp cymbals and hi-hats)

What you'll hear: The guitar riffs hit harder, and the snare drum cracks with authority. Common error: Over-boosting 230Hz makes the mix sound boxy and congested.

## Step 5: Jazz Music Settings

Jazz is all about natural acoustic instruments and subtle details. In Volume Booster - Sound Booster, dial in:
- 60Hz: +1dB (gentle warmth for upright bass)
- 230Hz: +2dB (richness for saxophone and piano)
- 910Hz: +3dB (clarity for brass and reeds)
- 3.6kHz: +2dB (presence for vocals and solos)
- 14kHz: +1dB (light shimmer for cymbals)

What you'll hear: Instruments separate beautifully, with each note breathing naturally. Common error: Boosting 14kHz too high introduces harshness that ruins the organic feel.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Boosting all bands equally**: This creates a flat, lifeless sound and can cause distortion. Use subtle boosts (1-3dB) on most bands, with only one or two bands getting +4dB or more.
2. **Ignoring the 910Hz band**: This midrange band is critical for vocal clarity. Neglecting it results in muffled, unclear vocals across all genres.
3. **Over-boosting bass**: +6dB at 60Hz is plenty for hip-hop. Going higher causes speaker rattle and ear fatigue within minutes.
4. **Using headphones as reference**: Headphones have different frequency responses. Always test your equalizer settings on your primary playback device (speakers or car stereo).
5. **Not adjusting for track variations**: A pop song from 2010 may sound different from a 2024 hit. Save multiple presets for sub-genres if needed.
