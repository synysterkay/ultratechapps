## Why Your Bass Sounds Like a Wet Blanket (And It's Not Your Headphones)

You know that moment when you queue up a bass-heavy track — maybe "HUMBLE." by Kendrick Lamar or "Bad Guy" by Billie Eilish — and instead of feeling the kick drum in your chest, you get a vague, buzzing wall of sound? You check your headphones. You check your phone. You try a different streaming app. Nothing helps.

Here's what's actually happening. Most smartphone audio systems and default music app EQs apply what's called a **broadband bass boost** — they crank everything from 60 Hz to 250 Hz. The problem? Frequencies between 150 and 250 Hz are where *mud* lives. Push those up and your kick drum, bass guitar, and synth bass all smear together into an indistinguishable blob.

According to a 2022 study in the *Journal of the Audio Engineering Society*, listeners rated bass-heavy mixes with a **-4 dB dip at 200–300 Hz** as 31% "punchier" than the same mixes with a flat curve. The research is clear: punch isn't about volume. It's about *separation*.

But here's the frustrating part. Apple's built-in EQ presets on iOS? "Bass Boost" just raises everything below 200 Hz. Spotify's equalizer? Same story. And most Android phones don't even ship with a usable EQ at all. You're left with one blunt instrument trying to sculpt a sound that needs surgical precision.

If you've ever felt like your music was *almost* there — punchy enough to feel exciting but just shy of perfect — you're not imagining it. You're fighting a tool that wasn't designed for the job.
