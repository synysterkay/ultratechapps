### Step 4: Shape the EQ for Speaker Clarity (2 minutes)

Tap **Equalizer** and choose the **“Speaker” preset** if available, or set these bands manually:

- **60 Hz:** +2 dB (adds warmth without mud)
- **250 Hz:** -1 dB (removes boxiness)
- **1 kHz:** +3 dB (vocal clarity)
- **4 kHz:** +2 dB (presence)
- **12 kHz:** +1 dB (air)

This curve is based on the **equal-loudness contour** research by Fletcher-Munson, which shows human hearing is less sensitive to lows and highs at low volumes. Lifting mids makes the phone sound louder at the same physical output.  
**Common error:** Boosting 60 Hz too much causes rattling — keep bass moves small on phone speakers.

### Step 5: Enable Bass Boost and Virtualizer — Carefully (1 minute)

Toggle **Bass Boost** to **30%** and **Virtualizer** to **20%**. These add perceived fullness, but too much bass boost is the #1 cause of blown phone speakers.  
**What you’ll see:** A small “Stereo Widening” indicator appears.  
**Common error:** On mono phone speakers, virtualizer can cause phase weirdness — if it sounds hollow, drop it to 10%.

### Step 6: Run the 440 Hz Safety Test (1 minute)

Open a tone generator and play **440 Hz at your boosted setting for 20 seconds**. Listen for buzz, rattle, or breakup. If you hear any, reduce the booster by **5%** and retest.  
**Why it matters:** A pure tone exposes distortion that music masks. I caught a rattle at +35% on an older Galaxy this way — saved me a repair.

## 5 Common Mistakes That Damage Speakers (And Fixes)

1. **Running boost at 100% system volume.** Fix: keep system volume at 70–80% and let the app do the lifting.
2. **Maxing bass boost on phone speakers.** Small drivers can’t move enough air; +30% is the safe ceiling.
3. **Ignoring distortion as “just loudness.”** Crackle is clipping — back off 5% immediately.
4. **Using boost for hours on end.** Limit boosted sessions to 30 minutes, then take a 5-minute break.
5. **Skipping the tone test.** A 20-second 440 Hz check catches damage-causing rattle before your ears do.
