### Step 2: Understand the Basic EQ Bands
An equalizer splits sound into different frequency bands. Adjusting these bands changes the tonal balance. Typically, you have:
*   **Low Frequencies (Bass):** 60Hz - 250Hz. Controls the thump and power.
*   **Low-Mid Frequencies:** 250Hz - 2kHz. Affects the body of instruments and vocals.
*   **High-Mid Frequencies:** 2kHz - 8kHz. Governs the clarity and presence of sounds.
*   **High Frequencies (Treble):** 8kHz - 16kHz. Adds sparkle and airiness.

## Common Equalizer Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Over-Boosting the Bass:** Cranked bass might feel exciting for a minute, but it drowns out mids and highs, causes distortion, and can damage your speakers. Use the Bass Booster feature judiciously.
2.  **The "Extreme V" Curve:** Slamming all sliders to the top and bottom creates a hollow, unnatural sound with no mid-range body. Vocals and core instruments will disappear.
3.  **Not Saving Presets:** Manually resetting your EQ for every song is a hassle. Failing to save your perfect custom settings in the app is the fastest way to forget what worked.
4.  **Ignoring Volume Levels:** When you boost certain frequencies, the overall volume can increase and lead to clipping (digital distortion). Ensure the master volume in the app and on your device is at a safe, clear level.
5.  **Using One Setting for Everything:** This is the core problem we're solving. Applying your heavy bass hip-hop EQ to a podcast will make the speaker's voice sound boomy and unclear.
