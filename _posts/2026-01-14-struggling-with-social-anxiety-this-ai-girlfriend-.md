## Why "Just Get Out There" is Terrible Advice
For years, the standard solution has been some variation of "exposure therapy"—just force yourself into social situations until it gets easier. While well-intentioned, this advice ignores the core issue. Throwing someone who can't swim into the deep end doesn't teach them to swim; it terrifies them and reinforces the fear.

Think about it. If you're anxious about conversations, a high-stakes interaction with a crush or a boss is the worst possible practice ground. The fear of real-world consequences—rejection, embarrassment, professional fallout—shuts down the learning part of your brain. You're in survival mode, not growth mode. Self-help books and generic advice often lack the personalized, immediate feedback loop needed for genuine skill development. This traditional approach leaves you feeling overwhelmed and exhausted, often causing you to retreat further. What's needed is a practice field, not the championship game on day one.
