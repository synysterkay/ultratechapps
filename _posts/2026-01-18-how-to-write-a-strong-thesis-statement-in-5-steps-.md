## 5 Common Thesis Statement Mistakes to Avoid

Even with a great tool, knowing what *not* to do is key. Avoid these pitfalls:

1.  **The Fact, Not an Argument:** "Solar power is a renewable energy source." This is a fact, not a thesis. A thesis must be something you can argue for or against.
2.  **The Vague Announcement:** "This essay will discuss the benefits of solar energy." This states your topic but not your unique argument or perspective.
3.  **The Overly Broad Claim:** "Government policy affects the environment." This is too wide to cover meaningfully in a standard essay. A strong thesis has a narrow, manageable focus.
4.  **The List Thesis:** "Solar power is good because it's clean, renewable, and creates jobs." This becomes a list of points rather than a cohesive, argument-driven statement. Your thesis should imply an argument, not just a catalog.
5.  **The Unresearchable Opinion:** "Solar panels are the best thing ever." This is pure opinion without a clear path for evidence-based support. Your claim must be grounded in research and logic.
