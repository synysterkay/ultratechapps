## Why “Just Trust Your Gut” Isn’t Enough

For years, the standard advice has been: “Listen to your intuition” or “Ask your friends what they think.” While well-intentioned, these solutions have critical flaws when you’re in the thick of it.

**Your Gut Can Be Biased.** When you like someone, your brain is flooded with dopamine and oxytocin—the ‘feel-good’ and ‘bonding’ chemicals. This ‘honeymoon phase’ literally rewires your perception to overlook flaws and amplify positives. Your ‘gut’ is chemically encouraged to ignore those red flags.

**Friends Are Biased Too.** They love you and want you to be happy. They might downplay concerns to avoid hurting your feelings, or they might project their own dating experiences onto your situation. What you often need isn’t a cheerleader or a critic, but a neutral, objective observer.

**Online Articles Are Generic.** Googling ‘is he a narcissist?’ leads to a rabbit hole of extreme, clinical checklists that rarely match the nuanced, early-stage behavior you’re seeing. This can either cause unnecessary panic or provide false reassurance.

You’re left analyzing texts alone at midnight, with no clear, unbiased framework to understand what you’re experiencing. It’s an isolating and confusing place to be.
