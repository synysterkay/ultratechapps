### Step-by-Step Tutorial: How to Boost Audio on Your Phone

#### Step 1: Analyze Your Audio's Current Loudness
Before you boost, know what you're working with. Play your video or podcast and listen critically. Note the quietest and loudest parts. Use a free audio meter app (like Decibel X) to measure the average loudness. Most mobile recordings average around -20 to -15 dBFS (decibels full scale), while professional audio sits at -14 to -12 dBFS. This tells you how much boost you need.

#### Step 2: Choose the Right File and Format
Open your file in a simple editor like YouCut (for video) or Voice Recorder Pro (for audio). Trim any dead air at the start and end. Export in a high-quality format: AAC or MP3 at 192kbps or higher for podcasts, MP4 for video. Avoid overly compressed formats like low-bitrate MP3—they'll sound worse after boosting.

### Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

- **Mistake #1: Boosting to 100% immediately.** This causes severe distortion. Fix: Always start at 50% and work up in 10% increments.
- **Mistake #2: Ignoring the original loudness.** If your audio is already at -12 dBFS, a +200% boost will blow it out. Fix: Measure first and boost only what's needed.
- **Mistake #3: Not using headphones.** You can't judge audio quality on phone speakers. Fix: Use studio monitors or good earbuds.
- **Mistake #4: Skipping the limiter.** Even with a perfect boost, a sudden shout can clip. Fix: Always add a limiter at -2 dB.
- **Mistake #5: Boosting the entire track equally.** Quiet parts might need more boost than loud parts. Fix: Use dynamic compression (if available) or boost in sections.
