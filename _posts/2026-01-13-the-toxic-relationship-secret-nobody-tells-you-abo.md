## Why We Miss the Red Flags: The Psychology of Blind Spots

Our brains are wired for connection, not for forensic analysis of a potential partner's character. This biological drive, combined with societal pressures to be in a relationship, can create significant blind spots. Common psychological traps include:

*   **The Halo Effect:** When someone is physically attractive or charming, we unconsciously assume they are also kind, honest, and trustworthy.
*   **Trauma Bonding:** Intense push-pull dynamics create a chemical addiction in the brain, mistaking anxiety and intermittent reinforcement for love.
*   **Normalization:** If you grew up in a dysfunctional environment, certain toxic behaviors might feel familiar, and therefore "normal," even when they're harmful.

This is where objective insight becomes priceless. You can't always trust your gut when your gut has been conditioned by past experiences. You need a second opinion—one powered by data and psychology, not hope and hormones.
