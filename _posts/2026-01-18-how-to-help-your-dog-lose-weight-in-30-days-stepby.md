The most reliable initial assessment doesn't require a scale. First, the **Rib Check**: run your hands along your dog's sides. You should be able to easily feel their ribs under a thin layer of fat. If you have to press hard to find them, that's a red flag. Second, the **Profile Test**: look at your dog from the side. You should see a clear abdominal tuck—the area behind the ribs should slope upward. A straight or sagging profile from chest to hindquarters often indicates excess weight.

### Step 2: Document Key Behavioral Red Flags
Weight gain often shows in behavior before it's glaringly obvious on the body. Grab your notebook and jot down observations over 2-3 days. Is your dog reluctant to go on walks they once enjoyed? Do they get winded or lie down frequently during play? Do they snore loudly or seem lethargic most of the day? These behavioral signs, combined with the physical tests, create a clearer picture of their overall condition.

## 3 Common Mistakes That Sabotage Dog Weight Loss

1.  **Eyeballing Portions:** "A cup" of kibble can vary wildly. Using the same scoop for a high-calorie and a low-calorie food is a classic error. Always use a proper measuring cup and rely on PupShape's precise gram/ounce logging for accuracy.
2.  **Forgetting Treat Calories:** Those training treats, peanut butter in a Kong, and cheese slices for pills count! This is the #1 reason plans fail. Log every morsel in the app's tracker to stay within the daily budget.
3.  **Relying Solely on the Scale:** Weight can fluctuate. If the scale isn't moving but your dog's profile looks leaner and they're more energetic, you're still winning. The photo log in PupShape is essential for capturing this full-picture progress.
4.  **Changing Too Much Too Fast:** Suddenly slashing food and forcing intense exercise can backfire, causing nutrient deficiencies or injury. PupShape creates a gradual, sustainable plan that prioritizes your dog's health over rapid loss.
