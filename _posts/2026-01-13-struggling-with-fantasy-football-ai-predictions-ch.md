## Why Your Current Research Methods Are Failing You
The old playbook is broken. Relying on popular fantasy football tips from social media or a single expert’s opinion is like bringing a knife to a gunfight. These sources are often biased, reactive, and based on surface-level stats that don’t tell the whole story.

Manually comparing player stats across different websites is a tedious, error-prone process. You might look at goals scored, but are you factoring in expected goals (xG), underlying performance metrics, fixture difficulty, and a player’s historical performance against a specific opponent? Probably not, because no human can synthesize that much data for every single relevant player before a deadline. This piecemeal approach creates blind spots. You end up making emotional picks—like favoring a big-name player who is past his prime—or falling for last week’s points, a classic trap in fantasy sports analysis.
