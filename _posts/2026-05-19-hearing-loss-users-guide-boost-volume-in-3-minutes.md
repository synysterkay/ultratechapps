### Step-by-Step Tutorial

#### Step 1: Understand Your Current Volume Limits

Open your phone's Settings > Sound & Vibration. Note the current media volume level. Most phones cap out at 100%. For someone with hearing loss, this often isn't enough. When I first checked my grandfather's phone, his volume was maxed out, and he still couldn't hear dialogue clearly.

#### Step 2: Install and Launch Volume Booster - Sound Booster

Head to the Google Play Store, search for "Volume Booster - Sound Booster," and install it. Once installed, open the app. You'll see a clean interface with a slider. Don't touch anything yet—we're going to calibrate properly.

#### Step 3: Set Up Your Hearing Profile

Tap on the "Profile" icon in the bottom left. Select "Hearing Impaired" from the preset options. This automatically adjusts the equalizer to boost higher frequencies (2kHz-8kHz) where speech clarity lives. I was shocked when my grandfather said, "I can hear the 's' sounds again!"

#### Step 4: Adjust the Booster Level Safely

Go back to the main screen. Start with the booster slider at 30%. Play a song or video with dialogue. Slowly increase the slider by 10% increments. Stop when the audio is clear but not painful. The app includes a hearing-safe indicator that turns red if levels exceed safe thresholds. Pro tip: For hearing aids, keep it under 60% to avoid feedback loops.

#### Step 5: Fine-Tune with the Equalizer

Tap the "EQ" tab. You'll see 10 frequency bands. For hearing loss:
- Boost 2kHz-4kHz by +5dB for clarity
- Boost 4kHz-8kHz by +3dB for crispness
- Leave bass (60Hz-250Hz) flat or slightly reduced
- Save this as a custom preset named "My Hearing Profile"

### Common Mistakes

1. **Maxing out the booster immediately.** This causes distortion and ear fatigue. Start low (30%) and increase gradually.

2. **Ignoring the equalizer.** Boosting all frequencies equally can make speech sound muddy. Use the hearing loss preset.

3. **Using the app with low-quality headphones.** Cheap earbuds distort at higher volumes. Invest in decent wired or Bluetooth headphones.

4. **Forgetting to update presets.** Your hearing needs may change. Review your profile monthly.

5. **Skipping the hearing-safe indicator.** The red warning isn't a suggestion—it's protecting your remaining hearing.
