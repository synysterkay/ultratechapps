**A volume booster increases the digital gain of the audio signal, making it louder before it reaches your headphones or speaker. An amplifier, on the other hand, increases the power of the analog signal after it leaves your device. For most phone users, the bottleneck is the digital gain, not the hardware—so a volume booster can effectively bypass the phone's safe volume limit and deliver significantly louder audio without the need for extra hardware.**


This is the key insight I wish I'd known months ago. Your phone's default volume slider is set to a conservative level to prevent hearing damage and speaker distortion. But if you're using high-quality earbuds or headphones that can handle more power, you're leaving volume on the table. A good volume booster works by applying a digital gain to the audio stream, often using algorithms to prevent clipping and distortion, so you can safely push the volume higher.
